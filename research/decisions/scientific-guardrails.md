# Scientific guardrails

These guardrails apply to documents, code, experiments, and agent-generated proposals.

## Separate epistemic categories

Every important statement should be identifiable as established mathematics or physics, an implemented deterministic result, a physical hypothesis, a conjecture, or philosophical motivation. No category may silently inherit the authority of another.

## Parameterization is not discovery

A model does not become new physics merely because a new symbol, internal time coordinate, or state-dependent rate has been introduced. Physical novelty requires an invariant or operational distinction that survives admissible transformations. Likewise, a non-unit symbolic ratio between active tempo factors is only an obstruction to the simplest common reparameterization, not itself evidence of new physics.

## Survival is not validation

`survived` means only that one implemented falsification test did not destroy the claim. It is not evidence of proof, experimental confirmation, or superiority to established theory.

## Deterministic verdict authority

LLMs may propose hypotheses, counterexamples, transformations, invariants, and experiments. They may not certify proofs or produce the authoritative final verdict for an implemented test.

## Cheap falsification first

Before numerical scale is increased, prefer contract validation, dimensional checks, symbolic simplification, reparameterization analysis, symmetry/conservation checks, and known-limit regressions.

## Reproducibility before scale

No expensive computation should become central evidence until the same run can be replayed from immutable inputs with recorded code/dependency provenance.

## Explicit failure modes

Every experiment specification should say what result would falsify, survive, invalidate, or render the experiment inconclusive. Execution failure must never be converted into a favorable scientific verdict.

## Conservative scope expansion

Quantum, relativistic, cosmological, stochastic, PDE, or field-theoretic claims require new problem statements and invariance criteria. They must not inherit v0.1 conclusions by analogy.
